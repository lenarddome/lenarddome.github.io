I work on theories of learning and cognition: learning dynamics, generalisation, and the formal evaluation of network models. One of my central concern is model adequacy, meaning whether a model captures the right mechanism and not just the right outcome.

I study this through failure. Many mechanisms produce correct behaviour, but few produce the right error under the right conditions, so failures constrain theories far more sharply than successes do. My work traces irrational generalisation across supervised and unsupervised learning <a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.06149">arXiv:2608.06149</a>, and identifies an instability in attention-learning networks along with an architectural fix that applies to the whole class <a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.08615">arXiv:2609.08615</a>. I am keen on extending this work to any learning system.

I am also interested in what it reveals about when decision-making fails, both as a lens on psychiatric conditions and as a basis for systems that support rather than supplant human judgment.
